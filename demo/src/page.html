What the generator guarantees

Top-down briefs

The plan fixes the order of tools first. Worlds, regions, places and rooms each receive a brief: what the player is guaranteed to own on entry, and what must be found inside. Children never report anything a parent has to react to.

One builder at every level

Regions of a world and rooms of a dungeon are built by the same routine. It attaches one node at a time and may only lock an edge with something already owned, so the tree is solvable by construction. Loops and one-way ledges are added afterwards and kept only if the checks still pass.

Ammo, keys and rupees

Bombs and arrows are never counted. They are either renewable (bag or bow owned, and a refill reachable) or unusable. Every ammo gate gets a free refill on its near side. Small keys use worst-case logic, so no order of opening doors can waste one.

NPCs that move

An NPC is a list of stages, and the active one is the last whose condition holds. Conditions read only what the player owns, which never shrinks, so NPCs only move forward. A stage may not activate before its zone is reachable.

Layers that agree

Overworld, underworld and sky share one tile grid. Stairs sit at the same tile on both sides, and what lies above decides the cave below: lakes flood it, mountains fill it with crystal. Interiors are separate tile spaces, so a house can be larger inside.

Rivers, swimming and waterfalls

Rivers flow downhill along the lines between screens. On a region border they replace the rim; through a region they split it into two banks; in a cave the treasure sits on the far bank. With the Swim Gear a level bank lets you wade in and swim the whole stretch, and a drop in level is a one-way waterfall unless that could trap you, in which case it gets a climbing vine.

Tiles are proven, not trusted

After painting, walkable tiles are flood-filled into zones. Each gate must separate exactly the two zones of its edge, and each chest must stand in the zone the logic says. A map that disagrees is thrown away and the next attempt runs from a derived seed.
